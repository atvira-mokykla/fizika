<Block kind="explain" title="Three ideas, three different questions">



<Frames>


<Frame title="Temperature: how warm?">

Temperature describes the thermal state. In the particle model, for the same liquid, a higher temperature is associated with more intense thermal motion. A thermometer measures temperature after it approaches thermal equilibrium with the object. Touch is unreliable: metal and wood in the same room may feel different because they transfer energy to your hand at different rates.

</Frame>




<Frame title="Internal energy: what is stored?">

Internal energy includes the energy of the particles’ microscopic motion and interactions. It is a property of the system we choose, for example all the water in the cup. It is not the energy of the whole cup moving across a table. Mass, material, temperature and state all matter; a thermometer is not a meter of total internal energy.

</Frame>




<Frame title="Heat: what crosses the boundary?">

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. We use $Q$ for the transferred amount and measure it in joules (J). If warm and cool water exchange energy without external work, net heat flows from the warmer part towards the cooler part until equilibrium. Say “the water received heat”, rather than “the water contains heat”. Internal energy is stored; heat describes transfer.

</Frame>




<Frame title="Two ways to change internal energy">

Internal energy can change through heat transfer **or work**. A warm radiator transfers energy to cooler surroundings. Rubbing your hands or stirring a liquid with a paddle transfers energy by work. These processes can occur together: define the system and explain which transfer you are discussing.

</Frame>


</Frames>



**A scale is not a heating process.** 20 °C = 293.15 K; 35 °C = 308.15 K. The change is 15 °C = 15 K. Kelvin has no degree symbol. Changing units alone does not change the water.

</Block>

<Task id="g9-thermal-01-c5" outcome="g9.thermal.1">

Write two sentences correcting: “Rubbing my hands adds heat stored in them, and doubling their temperature doubles their internal energy.” Identify the transfer and explain the missing information.



<Marks>
<Mark pts={1}>Identify work as the transfer from rubbing.</Mark>
<Mark pts={1}>Distinguish stored internal energy from heat transfer.</Mark>
<Mark pts={1}>Reject a general proportionality based only on a temperature reading.</Mark>
</Marks>




<Solution>

Rubbing transfers energy by work, increasing the hands’ internal energy; heat is not something “stored” in them. A temperature reading alone does not determine total internal energy, and doubling a Celsius reading is not physically doubling absolute temperature. Even in kelvin, proportionality is not a general rule for arbitrary systems; it needs a specific model.

<Block kind="summary" title="Keep the question attached to the quantity">

**Temperature:** thermal state, °C or K. **Internal energy:** microscopic energy of the chosen system, J. **Heat:** energy transfer due to a temperature difference, J. Internal energy changes by heat transfer or work. A temperature difference of 1 °C equals 1 K; an absolute temperature needs the scale offset.
